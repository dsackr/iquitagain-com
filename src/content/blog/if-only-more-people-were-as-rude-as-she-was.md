When you go into a fast food restaurant, you expect the person behind the counter to say something like <tt>"Welcome to Good Burger, Home of the Good Burger, Can I take your order?"</tt>... but that isn't what I got.  She took my friend's order as expected but when I stepped up in line, the cashier said these words - <span style="font-size: medium;">"<span style="color: #993300;">You shouldn't eat here - you're getting fat!</span>"</span> I'm not making this up, people.

If your first thought was <span style="font-size: large;">"<span style="color: #ff0000;">OMG, how rude!</span>"</span> then you are in the same mind set I was when I heard the comment.  I couldn't believe it.  An employee of a major fast food restaurant actually said I was <span style="color: #ff6600;"><span style="font-size: x-large;">TOO FAT</span></span> to eat there.  I was a little embarrassed but laughed it off and ordered <span style="font-size: medium;"><span style="color: #000080;">two double cheese burgers, a six piece order of chicken nuggets, and a large diet coke</span></span> (totaling about $4).

After she filled my order, I took my tray and went to sit down with my much lighter friend and co-worker, Jason.  He was sitting in a booth {corporate groan from all fat people}.  Now if you aren't large, you may not know that a booth is difficult because you cannot move the seat out from the table to allow for the <em>additional required</em> room.  At this particular establishment, some of the booths are not evenly spaced so while my lighter friend had almost 2 feet of space between him and the table, when I went to sit down my gut was laying on the table and my butt was pushing the chair back so hard, <span style="color: #993300;"><span style="font-size: x-large;">I swear I heard it cry</span></span>.  Noticing my situation (likely because he was a bit more sensitive to it after hearing the comment from the cashier), Jason suggested we switch places... which I gladly excepted.

He got up, moved his tray to my side of the booth and I his, but as he sat down again to more than 2 feet of empty space between his stomach and the table, I realized this booth IS evenly spaced... As I squeeze into the both, <span style="color: #0000ff;"><span style="font-size: large;">pushing my gut under the table</span></span>, the <span style="font-size: x-large;">embarrassment</span> of it all was really starting to sink in.  Even though this happened over 5 years ago, <span style="font-size: medium;">I can't help but think about it</span> every now and again.

The truth is, <span style="color: #993300;"><span style="font-size: large;">that cashier was right</span></span> - I was (and still am) too fat to eat there.  If you read my <a href="/weightloss-articles/should-fat-people-just-suck-it-up">Should Fat People Just Suck it Up</a> post (worth reading again in my opinion), then you may have the impression that I want people to be more sensitive to us FAT folk; I do but not in the way you might be thinking.  I do want people to understand that many FAT people are <span style="color: #ff0000;"><span style="font-size: x-large;">food addicts</span></span> and treat them as they would an alcoholic (you wouldn't insist your AA friend drink a beer, <span style="color: #993300;"><span style="font-size: medium;">don't insist the FAT guy try your brownies</span></span>).  But I don't want people to feel like they shouldn't hurt a FAT persons feelings by saying "<span style="color: #000080;">Dude, are you sure about that second piece?</span>"

<span style="color: #993300;"><span style="font-size: x-large;">The truth is</span></span>, I wish more people were like that cashier.  What appeared rude at first glance, was actually very thoughtful.  I went to lunch there often and she had noticed that I was gaining weight.  We weren't friends - I do not know nor did I ever know her name - but she noticed I was gaining weight and said something about it.  My co-worker and close friend may not have noticed that I had packed on the poundage because he saw me everyday or maybe he did noticed but was to uncomfortable to say anything about it.  This woman saw me about once or twice a week; she noticed and she spoke up - <span style="font-size: medium;">like a good friend trying to grab you back from the cliffs edge</span> - she spoke up.

<span style="color: #000080;"><span style="font-size: large;">The only thing worse</span></span> than being ridiculed for being fat - is <span style="color: #ff0000;"><span style="font-size: x-large;">being invisible</span></span> for it.  Fat people are intentionally not noticed because of our weight; We get passed over for promotions in our work life, We are not invited to group functions in our social life, I've even been past over for volunteer opportunities in my church life... Being 300lbs and pointed at may be <strong><em>uncomfortable </em></strong>- but being 300lbs and <em><strong>being ignored</strong></em> is unbearable.  <span style="color: #0000ff;"><span style="font-size: x-large;">She noticed me</span></span>.  And she said something to <strong>help</strong> me even though it was tough for me to hear.  And five years later - despite the fact that I will most likely never run into that cashier again - I hope I'm making her proud.
